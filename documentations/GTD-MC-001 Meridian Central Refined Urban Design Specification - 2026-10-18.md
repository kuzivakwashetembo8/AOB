## Meridian Central — Refined Urban Design Specification

**Reference analysis incorporated**

The six references support an important refinement to our accepted proposal: **Meridian Central needs a different urban scale from South Ward, not merely taller buildings.**

South Ward is neighborhood-oriented. Meridian Central must accommodate concentrated vehicle traffic, larger pedestrian crowds, commercial entrances, transit stops, plazas and substantially taller buildings.

### 1. What the images establish

| Reference | Observed design characteristics | Application to Meridian Central |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Downtown street corner | Glass towers rising behind lower commercial structures, transit access, trees, traffic lights and street-level activity | Combine skyscrapers with 2–5-floor podiums, small shops, station entrances and public-facing ground floors |
| 2 — Towers and green plaza | Very tall buildings framing a wide landscaped pedestrian space, with cafés, seating and uninterrupted walking routes | B2 becomes the district's signature public plaza, not a small patch of grass |
| 3 — Architectural skyline | Varied tower silhouettes, glass and metal materials, broad junctions and distinct building crowns | Avoid uniform rectangular skyscrapers; incorporate setbacks, podiums, stepped roofs and distinctive landmarks |
| 4 — Game boulevard | Continuous trees, shaded footpaths, roadside bollards, vehicle lanes and mid-rise buildings | Add regular street furniture and consistent roadside planting without blocking pedestrian space |
| 5 — Game intersection | Multiple vehicle lanes, marked pedestrian crossings, landscaped median and visible traffic movement | Proper intersections with turning space, crosswalks, traffic signals and separated pedestrian paths |
| 6 — Green urban district | Vegetation between buildings, diverse rooflines, roof greenery and building clusters separated by open space | Use selective green rooftops, planted courtyards and a softer district boundary; do not make the commercial core into a forest |

The photographs cannot establish exact real-world dimensions without a known scale. The measurements below are therefore **proposed game-world design standards** derived from the spatial proportions and intended uses.

One distinction matters: when we say the city needs **wider curbs**, we mean wider sidewalks and pedestrian frontage. We do not need unusually tall concrete curb edges.

A 7 m commercial sidewalk, for example, should allocate roughly 1 m to building frontage, 4 m to unobstructed walking and 2 m to trees, lighting, benches and other street furniture. This is the pedestrian scale shown in the references.

## 5. The smaller details that make Meridian Central believable

| Urban component | Required implementation |
|---|---|
| **Building podiums** | Most tall towers need 2–5 floors of wider, lower structure at street level rather than glass walls extending identically from ground to roof |
| **Entrances** | Every building has a visible door; major commercial buildings get distinctive entrance geometry, taller ground floors and access paths |
| **Street-level commerce** | Cafés, retail, lobbies, banks and restaurants visibly face pedestrian sidewalks rather than blank perimeter walls |
| **Sidewalk furnishing zones** | Trees, bins, lamps, planters, benches and signs must sit outside clear walking paths |
| **Crosswalks** | Broad zebra crossings, stop lines, curb ramps and refuge islands where the crossing distance warrants them |
| **Intersections** | Signal poles, appropriate turning geometry, lane markings and non-overlapping pedestrian/collision paths |
| **Street trees** | Repeated species and spacing on boulevards, variation in the plaza and commercial side streets |
| **Loading infrastructure** | Delivery access behind shops, hotel drop-offs, bus bays, taxi areas and parking entrances |
| **Ground surfaces** | Asphalt roads; tiled/concrete paving for pedestrian zones; grass concentrated in plaza, medians and landscaped courts |
| **Rooftops** | Parapets, mechanical equipment, roof setbacks and selective green roofs; avoid identical flat cuboids |
| **Glass skyscrapers** | Glass materials with real three-dimensional mullions and depth at close range, matching GTD's previously established design direction |
| **Lighting** | Road lamps, pedestrian-scale lighting, entry lights and illuminated ground floors; maintain the accepted ambient night sky |
| **Visual landmarks** | Different crowns, tower proportions and materials so the skyline is recognizable while driving |

| Block | Street-level identity | Open-space emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Formal banking entrances and small commercial activity | Forecourts, controlled loading and pedestrian routes |
| A2 | Dense commercial towers and retail arcade | Recessed entries and coordinated tower plazas |
| A3 | Railway station, hotel and passenger movement | Very wide station frontage, bus/taxi waiting areas |
| B1 | Corporate financial streets | Banks, cafés and business-oriented pedestrian space |
| **B2** | Signature commercial heart | **Large pedestrian plaza and green promenade**, with towers framing the space |
| B3 | Police and municipal buildings | Public frontage separated from restricted service access |
| C1 | Transitional residential-commercial area | Trees, courtyards and more neighborhood-scaled buildings |
| C2 | Hotels, events and restaurants | Terraces, drop-off forecourts and evening activity |
| C3 | Transport and utility facilities | Purpose-built bus bays, service yards and safe passenger routes |

For MC-19, I would reserve a roughly **30 m × 80 m landscaped pedestrian spine** within a wider plaza arrangement. This is a proposed footprint, not an addition to the approved site count. It gives the district the recognizable green open space shown in reference 2, with pedestrian circulation, trees, seating and active ground-floor frontages around it.

## 7. Final district-scale footprint

| Proposed district envelope | City block land parcel |
|---|---|
| **450–500 m** | **100–125 m** |
| Approx. each axis | Typical depth or width |

| Road network | Built environment |
|---|---|
| **8** | **36** |

This is substantially larger than the currently established South Ward playable area, making world streaming, building LOD, instanced street props and preservation of the existing 60-second startup target mandatory engineering constraints.

**Proposed refinement for acceptance:** Keep the original 36-site narrative register and eight street names, but adopt the wider road/sidewalk standards, varied architectural massing, podiums, pedestrian plaza and detailed streetscape requirements above as the physical design baseline.

This gives us a defined expected visual output before translating Meridian Central into the game's city-generation and rendering systems.
